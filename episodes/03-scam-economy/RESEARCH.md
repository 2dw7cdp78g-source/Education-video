# Episode 03: Research notes and fact-check

## First-hand source
- "The Joom Online Task Scam in Myanmar (2022): A First-Hand Account" (one participant, June–October 2022).
  - Fake site copied the name of Joom, a real, legitimate e-commerce platform. **The video says this clearly, to avoid confusing or defaming the real company.**
  - Entry deposit 28,000 kyat (~$15), rising to $30, then $70 (July 2022). Five levels; rebates from three levels of recruits.
  - Withdrawals stopped on **18 October 2022** ("pending", "rejected", "technical issues").
  - Koala: similar scheme, kyat and KBZPay, deposits from 10,000 to over 1,000,000 kyat, collapsed at the same time.
  - National losses: "many thousands of lakh kyat" (victims' estimate). The video says "more than a million US dollars", **marked as unverified**.
  - The participant later lost about $500 in similar schemes.
- Screenshots supplied by the author. **Blurred:** account name, invitation code, profile photo, team members' phone numbers, Facebook friends' photos.

## Online gambling section
From the author's Burmese-language article (tricks used against winners, RTP/house edge, agent commission chain, slot-machine psychology). Kept to **how players are cheated and how to recognise it**. Technical build details (databases, locking, biometrics) were left out on purpose.

## Wording choices for safety
- "Junta covering the scammers" is stated through attributed sources: the BGF/KNA is a junta ally; the US Treasury says it kept cooperating with the junta; locals and experts called the raid staged; no BGF leaders arrested. The conclusion says those in power "have looked away".
- Trafficking abuses are described in words only. No violent images.
