# Myanmar Stories: Launch Plan

A faceless, narrated documentary channel about Myanmar. It's in English with Burmese subtitles and aimed at international viewers, students of Southeast Asia, and people who want to help Myanmar refugees and the diaspora in Thailand.

---

## 1. The concept in one paragraph

You do the research and record the narration in your own voice. Claude Code helps turn that into a finished video. It fact-checks and tightens the script, builds the visuals (maps, timelines, charts, title cards, captions), times the English and Burmese subtitles to your voice, and assembles a ~10-minute video plus 2–3 short vertical clips cut from it. Those clips go to YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Facebook Reels and Instagram Reels. The tone is calm and explanatory, with no graphic violence.

## 2. Content pillars

| Pillar | Example topics |
|---|---|
| **The 2021 coup & civil war** | How the coup happened; the NUG, PDFs and ethnic armed organisations; Operation 1027; conscription law; who controls what (map explainer) |
| **Scam compounds** | How the compounds work; who runs and protects them (incl. military-aligned militias); trafficking victims' path in; **"How to spot a pig-butchering scam"** (useful to viewers, very shareable) |
| **Rural & humanitarian crisis** | Displacement, food insecurity, collapse of health care, disease outbreaks, the 2025 earthquake response, landmines (described, not shown) |
| **Refugees & diaspora in Thailand** | Life in Mae Sot and the border camps; legal status of migrant workers; **how viewers can help** (vetted organisations) |
| **Everything else about Myanmar** | History (colonial era → independence → 1962 → 1988 → 2007 → 2015), ethnic diversity, culture, food, religion, economy |

Mix the pillars over time. The "how to help" and "how to spot scams" videos tend to be the easiest to share. The history explainers build long-term search traffic.

### First 5 videos (suggested order)
1. **"Myanmar's civil war explained in 10 minutes"**: the anchor video everything else links back to.
2. **"Inside Myanmar's scam compounds"**: high-interest topic with a "how to protect yourself" ending.
3. **"Who are Myanmar's refugees in Thailand, and how can you help?"**
4. **"How the 2021 coup happened, day by day"**
5. **"Myanmar's forgotten rural crisis"**

Each long video gives you 2–3 shorts, so 5 videos give you about 12 shorts.

## 3. Safety & security (read this before posting anything)

The military government has arrested people for social-media posts, and it has pressured family members of critics living abroad. Scam syndicates are violent criminal organisations. Treat security as part of production.

### Protect yourself and your family
- **Decide on anonymity now.** It's hard to undo later. If you have family in Myanmar, or might travel there or to border areas, run the channel under a **brand name**, not your real name, and don't show your face.
- Create a **separate email account** just for the channel. Don't link it to your personal Facebook, phone contacts, or real name.
- Your voice could identify you to people who know you. If that's a risk, consider light voice processing.
- Don't film or describe your home, street, or anything identifying in the background.
- Remove location metadata from any photos you took yourself (Claude can do this in the pipeline).

### Protect your accounts (channels get hacked constantly)
- Turn on **2-step verification with an authenticator app or security key** (not SMS) on every platform and the channel email.
- **The #1 way YouTube channels get stolen is fake "sponsorship" emails** with an attachment or download link (a "contract", "video brief", or "game to review"). Never download or run files from sponsors. Real sponsors don't need you to install anything.
- Use a password manager. Don't reuse passwords.
- Add a trusted second person as a backup manager later. Don't share your password with anyone.

### Protect your sources
- Never show faces, names, villages, or unit names of interviewees unless they clearly agree **and** you've judged the risk yourself. Blur faces and alter voices by default.
- Don't publish details that could identify a scam-compound survivor or a refugee's legal status.
- **Do not contact, infiltrate, or "bait" scam operations.** Report on them using published investigations, court documents, and survivor testimony that's already public or given with consent.

### Legal & accuracy safety
- When naming individuals or companies (especially in scam-compound videos), only state what credible published sources say, and attribute it ("according to a UN report…").
- Keep a sources list for every video (see section 5) and link sources in the description.

## 4. Ethics & honesty rules for the channel

- **No AI-generated "photos" or "footage" of real events, places, or people.** For news and conflict content, realistic fake images destroy trust and can spread as misinformation. Use AI for **clearly illustrative** things (stylised illustrations, abstract backgrounds) and label them.
- Disclose AI use. YouTube, TikTok and Meta all require you to flag realistic AI-generated or altered content when uploading.
- No graphic violence. Describe events with care and use maps, data and illustration instead of gore. This also keeps videos eligible for ads.
- Include a corrections line in every description, and pin a comment if you get something wrong.

## 5. Production workflow (what you do vs. what Claude does)

```
 ① Research (you)        → notes, links, PDFs into research/<video>/
 ② Script (you + Claude) → Claude drafts/tightens a ~1,400-word script (~10 min),
                           fact-checks claims, lists sources
 ③ Record voice (you)    → read the script into your phone or a USB mic
 ④ Audio cleanup (Claude)→ noise reduction, level normalising (-14 LUFS)
 ⑤ Captions (Claude)     → transcribe your voice with timings → English .srt
                           → translate to Burmese .srt → YOU review the Burmese
 ⑥ Visuals (Claude)      → maps, timelines, charts, quote cards, title cards,
                           licensed photos with slow zoom, animated text
 ⑦ Assemble (Claude)     → 16:9 long video + 9:16 shorts with burned-in Burmese
                           subtitles, thumbnail drafts, titles & descriptions
 ⑧ Review & upload (you) → watch it fully, then upload to each platform
```

### Important things to know
- **Claude Code doesn't make photos or video footage by itself.** What it *can* do well, and automatically, is code-generated visuals: animated maps, data charts, timelines, text animations, and editing your audio and images together. That suits a documentary explainer style. If you want illustrated or AI images too, you'd add an image tool later (optional, about $10–30/month).
- **Burmese subtitles:** Claude can draft the Burmese translation, but **a fluent Burmese speaker (you) must check every line.** Machine translation of political and historical terms can be wrong or awkward. Use **Unicode Burmese** (not Zawgyi) with a proper Myanmar font (e.g. Noto Sans Myanmar) so the text displays correctly everywhere.
  - **YouTube long videos:** upload the Burmese `.srt` as a subtitle track (and an English one), and burn Burmese into the video too if you want it always visible.
  - **TikTok, Reels and Shorts:** burn the Burmese subtitles into the video, because subtitle files aren't well supported there.
- **Visual sources that are safe to use:** your own photos; Wikimedia Commons (check each licence); free stock (Pexels, Pixabay); public-domain maps; charts Claude builds from published data. **Never** download news footage from YouTube or TV and reuse it, or you'll get copyright strikes.

### Good research sources
UN (OHCHR, OCHA, UNODC on scam centres), ACLED (conflict data), Human Rights Watch, Amnesty, International Crisis Group, Reuters, AP, BBC, and Myanmar outlets like Frontier Myanmar, The Irrawaddy, Myanmar Now and DVB. Rule: **two independent credible sources for every factual claim**, and one primary source when possible.

## 8. Making money (honest expectations)

Most educational channels take **6–18 months** before earning meaningful money. Build several income streams:

1. **YouTube Partner Program:** ads unlock at 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days). Lower tiers (fan funding like memberships and Super Thanks) unlock earlier in many countries. Conflict topics sometimes get "limited ads", so keep it non-graphic and calm.
2. **TikTok Creator Rewards / Facebook monetisation:** availability depends on your country, so check what you qualify for.
3. **Viewer support:** Patreon, Ko-fi or YouTube memberships. Documentary audiences often support creators directly.
4. **Sponsorships:** later on, from education-related brands (language apps, VPNs, books). Only accept ones that fit and never install their files (see section 3).
5. **Grants & commissions:** journalism and human-rights funders sometimes support independent explainers. NGOs may pay for explainer videos.
6. **Licensing:** translated or subtitled versions for schools or organisations.

Keep "how to help" links (refugee organisations) separate from your own income links, and be transparent about which is which. Trust is your main asset.
